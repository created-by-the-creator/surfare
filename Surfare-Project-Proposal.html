Confidence does not protect people. Most adults feel able to spot a scam, many still fall for one, and the large majority of victims report harm to their mental well-being (PNA, 2026).

Today's tools leave people alone at the moment it counts

They need a strong signal

Many protection tools require a stable internet connection, so they fail when someone is on weak data or no data.

They speak in jargon

Results arrive in technical language that people without a cybersecurity background cannot act on.

They only look at links

Messages and images, where many scams actually start, are overlooked.

The need: a safety tool that works at a basic level offline, checks online when it can, and explains itself in simple language.

Meet Surfare: offline-first, online-enhanced

A web app that checks suspicious messages, links, images, and QR codes, gives a simple risk level, explains the warning signs, and recommends safe next steps.

    Nine features, one calm guide

    A camera coach built on how light behaves

    Before a photo or QR scan is submitted, Surfare checks blur, low light, and glare, then tells the user how to fix them. Move the sliders to see it work.

    Built for people who need plain answers

    Primary users

    Adults who need accessible guidance in recognizing potential scams and digital deception.

    Secondary users

    • Junior high school, senior high school, and college students who meet phishing, online fraud, and AI-generated content
    • Parents and guardians
    • Teachers and community digital-literacy advocates

    What the project sets out to do

    The general objective is to build an offline-first, online-enhanced web app that assesses suspicious messages, links, images, or videos, explains why content is flagged, and gives safety guidance so users can recognize similar threats later.

    Honest about privacy and limits

    Privacy first

    Surfare limits what it collects and keeps, validates file types and sizes, and tells users before anything may be sent to an outside threat-checking service.

    It says when it is unsure

    When the evidence is not enough to call something safe, suspicious, or malicious, Surfare says so instead of guessing.

    Tested with real people

    The evaluation covers offline and online behavior, response time, file handling, and how well users understand the explanations and advice.
